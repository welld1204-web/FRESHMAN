1.In what way are isotopes of a given element always different? In what way(s) are they always the same?


Answer

Isotopes of the same element differ in that they have the same number of protons and electrons but a different number of neutrons. Elements are defined by their number of protons; hence two elements with the same number of protons are considered the same.


Step by step solution


01 Definition of proton:

Positively charged particles are Protons which are located in the nucleus of an atom. The electromagnetic force separates the protons, while the strong force, which is stronger at short distances, draws them together.


02 Relation between proton and neutron:

A proton's charge is exactly equal to and opposite to the charge on an electron. As a result, in a neutral atom, the number of protons is always equal to the number of electrons.


03 Definition of neutron:

Neutrons, along with positively charged protons, are neutrally charged particles found in the nucleus of atoms. The electromagnetic force pushes the protons apart, but the strong force, which is greater across short distances, pulls them closer.


04 Definition of electron:

Electrons are negatively charged particles that reside as a cloud around an atom's nucleus. They are unbelievably little, so minuscule that quantum mechanics is required to explain their odd behaviour , and they are, as far as scientists can tell, a fundamental particle.

5. The following are the properties of isotopes of two elements that are essential in our diet. Determine the number of protons, neutrons and electrons in each and name them.

(a) atomic number 26, mass number 58, charge of 2+

(b) atomic number 53, mass number 127, charge of 1-


Answer:

a) Protons = 26, neutrons = 32, electrons = 24

b) Protons = 53, neutrons = 74, electrons = 54

Explanation:

For neutral atoms:

Atomic Number (Z)= number of protons = number of electrons-----(1)

Mass number (A) = number of protons + number of neutrons-----(2)

For ions with positive net charge:

Number of electrons = Z - net charge -----(3)

For ions with negative net charge:

Number of electrons = Z + net charge ----(4)

a) Z = 26, A = 58, Charge = +2

From equation (1):

Number of protons = 26

From equation (2)

Number of neutrons = A-Z = 58-26 = 32

From equation (3):

Number of electrons = Z - 2 = 26-2 = 24

b) Z = 53, A = 127, Charge = +1

From equation (1):

Number of protons = 53

From equation (2)

Number of neutrons = A-Z = 127-53 = 74

From equation (4):

Number of electrons = Z + 1 = 53+1 = 54

16. Using the periodic table, predict whether the following chlorides are ionic or covalent: KCl, NCl3, ICl, MgCl2, PCl5, and CCl4.


Answer

An ionic compounds formed by donation of electrons between the atom usually occurs between metals and nonmetals whereas a covalent compounds formed by sharing of electrons.

KCl is a ionic compound as bond between metals and nonmetals usually ionic.

NCl3 is not ionic, so it is covalent compound.

ICl contains two nonmetal atoms and thus would be bonded covalently.

MgCl2 is a ionic compound as bond between metal(Mg) and nonmetal(Cl) is ionic.

PCl5 is covalent compound.

CCl4 is a covalent compound since it features four nonpolar covalent bonds between carbon and chlorine.


17. For each of the following pairs of ions, write the symbol for the formula of the compound they will form:

(a) Ca²⁺, S²⁻

(b) NH₄⁺ , SO₄²⁻

(c) Al³⁺, Br−

(d) Na⁺ , HPO₄²⁻

(e) Mg²⁺ , PO₄³⁻


Answer

The ionic compounds formed are: CaS, (NH₄)₂SO₄, AlBr₃, Na₂HPO₄, and Mg₃(PO₄)₂. The subscripts in each compound's formula represent the charge of the other pairing ion as ionic compounds are electrically neutral.

Explanation:

The formulas of the compounds formed from the given pairs of ions are:

(a) CaS  for Ca²⁺, S²⁻,

(b) (NH₄)₂SO₄ for NH₄⁺, SO₄²⁻,

(c) AlBr₃ for Al³⁺, Br−,

(d) Na₂HPO₄ for Na⁺, HPO₄²⁻

(e) Mg₃(PO₄)₂ for Mg²⁺ , PO₄³⁻.

In every case, the subscript of each ion in the compound is the absolute value of the charge of the other ion. This principle is derived from the fact that ionic compounds are electrically neutral.

(b) FeSO4

(d) MgO

(e) NaNO2

answer

The name of Ca (H₂PO₄)₂ is calcium hydrogen phosphate. FeSO₄ is called ferrous sulphate, CaCO₃ is calcium carbonate and MgO is magnesium oxide, NaNO₂ is called sodium nitrite and KI is called potassium iodide.

What are ionic compounds?

Ionic compounds are formed between metals and non-metals. Metals are electron rich and can easily donate electrons to other metals. From metals to non-metals the electronegativity increases.

Non-metals deficient of electrons tends to gain electrons and easily forms anions. Thus metals donate electrons to nonmetals forming ionic compounds.

The name of the inorganic compound Ca (H₂PO₄)₂ is calcium hydrogen phosphate. FeSO₄ is called ferrous sulphate.

CaCO₃ is called calcium carbonate and MgO is magnesium oxide, NaNO₂ is called sodium nitrite and KI is called potassium iodide.


21 (22). What are the IUPAC names of the following compounds?

A. Manganese dioxide

B. Mercurous chloride (Hg2Cl2)

C. Ferric nitrate [Fe(NO3)3]

C. Titanium Tetrachloride

D. Cupric bromide (CuBr2)


Answer

  1. The IUPAC name of manganese dioxide is Manganese(iv)oxide.
  2. The IUPAC name of mercurous chloride is chloromercury. Or Mercury(I) chloride
  3. The IUPAC name of ferric nitrate is iron(III)nitrate.
  4. The IUPAC name of Titanium Tetrachloride is titanium(IV) chloride.
  5. The IUPAC name of Cupric bromide is copper(II)bromide.


Step by step solution

1. Follow the rules of naming the compounds

  • If the compound is a salt, cation is named first then anion is named.
  • A Roman number is added in the bracket to specify the oxidation state of metal.
  • If the metal is anionic complex ion, use the suffix -ate.
  • Ligand is named before the central metal atom.
  • If more than one ligand is there, they are named in alphabetical order.

2. Determine the IUPAC names

  1. The IUPAC name of manganese dioxide is Manganese(iv)oxide.
  2. The IUPAC name of mercurous chloride is chloromercury. Or Mercury(I) chloride
  3. The IUPAC name of ferric nitrate is iron(III)nitrate.
