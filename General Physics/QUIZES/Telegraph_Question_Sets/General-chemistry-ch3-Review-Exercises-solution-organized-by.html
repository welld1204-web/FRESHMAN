17. A compound of carbon and hydrogen contains 92.3 percent c and has a molar mass of 78.1g/mol. What is its molecular formula?


Answer:

C₆H₆

Explanation:

We need to find the molecular formula of a compound of carbon (C) and hydrogen (H), so what we need to find out is the number of atoms of C and of H in the molecule. We know:

  • molar mass = 78.1 g/mol
  • C% = 92.3% = 92.3 g C / 100 g compound

So, in 1 mol of compound, 92.3% of the mass corresponds to Carbon:

mass of C / mol of compound = molar mass × C% = 78.1 g/mol × 92.3/100 = 72.1 g/mol

moles of C = mass C / molar mass C = 72.1 g / 12.011 g/mol

moles of C = 6 moles of C per mol of compound

If 72.1 g in a mol of compound are Carbon atoms, the difference between the molar mass and the mass of Carbon atoms will correspond to H atoms in 1 mol of compound:

mass of H / mol of compound = molar mass - mass of C/mol

mass of H = 78.1 g / mol - 72.1 g /mol = 6.0 g/mol of compound

moles of H = mass H / molar mass H = 6.0 g / 1.008 g/mol

moles of H = 6.0 moles of H per mol of compound

So one mol of compound has 6 moles of C and 6 moles of H.

The molecular formula is then written as C₆H₆

19. Determine the empirical and molecular formula for chrysotile asbestos. Chrysotile has the following percent composition: 28.03% Mg, 21.60% Si, 1.16% H, and 49.21% O. The molar mass for chrysotile is 520.8 g/mol.


Answer

To determine the empirical and molecular formula for a compound, we first need to convert the percent composition to moles. Then, we divide each element's mole value by the smallest mole value to get the ratio of atoms. This gives us the empirical formula. To find the molecular formula, we compare the empirical formula mass to the given molar mass.


Step 1: Convert Percent Composition to Moles

We assume a 100g sample, so the percent composition becomes grams. The molar mass of Mg is 24.31 g/mol, Si is 28.09 g/mol, H is 1.01 g/mol, and O is 16.00 g/mol.

  • Moles of Mg = 28.03g / 24.31g/mol = 1.15 mol
  • Moles of Si = 21.60g / 28.09g/mol = 0.77 mol
  • Moles of H = 1.16g / 1.01g/mol = 1.15 mol
  • Moles of O = 49.21g / 16.00g/mol = 3.08 mol


Step 2: Determine the Ratio of Atoms

We divide each mole value by the smallest mole value (0.77 mol) to get the ratio of atoms.

  • Ratio of Mg = 1.15 / 0.77 = 1.5
  • Ratio of Si = 0.77 / 0.77 = 1
  • Ratio of H = 1.15 / 0.77 = 1.5
  • Ratio of O = 3.08 / 0.77 = 4

The ratio of Mg and H is not a whole number, so we multiply all ratios by 2 to get whole numbers.

  • Ratio of Mg = 1.5 * 2 = 3
  • Ratio of Si = 1 * 2 = 2
  • Ratio of H = 1.5 * 2 = 3
  • Ratio of O = 4 * 2 = 8

So, the empirical formula is Mg3Si2H3O8.


Step 3: Determine the Molecular Formula

The empirical formula mass is (324.31) + (228.09) + (31.01) + (816.00) = 260.4 g/mol.

The ratio of the molar mass to the empirical formula mass is 520.8 g/mol / 260.4 g/mol = 2.

We multiply the subscripts in the empirical formula by this ratio to get the molecular formula: Mg6Si4H6O16.

21. What does it mean when we say that a 200-mL sample and a 400-mL sample of a solution of salt have the same molarity? In what ways are the two samples identical? In what ways are these two samples different?


Answer:

A 200−mL sample and a 400−mL sample can have the same molarity if they contain equal number of moles per volume of solution. The two samples are identical in concentration but they have different amount of salt and volume of solvent.





22.  Determine the molarity for each of the following solutions:

(a) 0.444 mol of CoCl2 in 0.654 L of solution

(b) 98.0 g of phosphoric acid, H3PO4, in 1.00 L of solution

(c) 0.2074 g of calcium hydroxide, Ca(OH)2, in 40.00 mL of solution

(d) 10.5 kg of Na2SO4∙10H2O in 18.60 L of solution

(e) 7.0×10-3 mol of I2 in 100.0 mL of solution

(f) 1.8 × 104 mg of HCl in 0.075 L of solution


Answer

a) 0.679 M

b) 1.010 M

c) 4.800 x 10-5 M

d) 1.752 M

e) 7 x 10-2 M

f) 6.66 M


Step by step solution


01Subpart a)

Molarity = Moles of solute / Liters of solution

Moles of solute = 0.444 mol

Liters of solution= 0.654 L

Molarity = 0.444mol/ 0.654 L = 0.679 M


02Subpart b)

98.0 g of phosphoric acid, H3PO4, in 1.00 L of solution

Mass = 98 g

Moles = Mass / Molecular weight

Molecular weight of H3PO4 = 3 x 1 + 30 + 4 x 16 = 33+ 64 = 97 g/mol

Moles = 98 g/ 97 g/mol = 1.010 mole of H3PO4

Molarity = 1.010 mole / 1 L = 1.010 M


03Subpart c)

0.2074 g of calcium hydroxide, Ca(OH)2, in 40.00 mL of solution

Mass = 0.2074 g

Molecular weight of Ca(OH)4 = 40 + 4 x 16 + 4 x 1= 40 + 64+4 = 108 g/mol

Moles = 0.2074 g/ 108 g/mol = 0.00192 mole of Ca(OH)4

Molarity = 0.00192 mol / 40 L = 4.800 x 10 -5 M


04Subpart d)

10.5 kg of Na2SO4∙10H2O in 18.60 L of solution

Mass = 10.5 kg = 10500 g ( 1kg = 1000g)

The molecular weight of Na2SO4∙10H2O = 2 x 23 + 32 + 14 x 16 + 20 x 1 = 322. 2 g/mol.

Moles of Na2SO4∙10H2O = 10500 g / 322. 2 g/mol = 32.588 mol

Molarity = 32.588 mol/ 18.60 L = 1.752 M


05Subpart e)

Moles = 7 x 10-3

Liters of solution = 100 mL = 0.1 L ( 1L = 1000 mL)

Molarity = 7 x 10-3 Mole/ 0.1 L

= 7 x 10-2 M


06Subpart f)

1.8×104 mg of HCl in 0.075 L of solution

Mass = 1.8×104 mg = 18 g ( 1g = 1000 mg )

Molecular weight of HCl = 1 + 35 = 36 g/mol

Moles of HCl = 18 g/ 36 g/mol = 0.5 mol

Molarity = 0.5 mol/ 0.075 L = 6.66 M
